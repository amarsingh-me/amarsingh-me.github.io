# AI writing tells: full catalog

Sources: Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup), tropes.fyi, Forbes "15 New Giveaway Signs of AI Writing" (May 2026) and "The 10 Giveaway Signs of AI Writing, Wikipedia Reveals" (Sept 2025), the oliviacal.com AI words blacklist, Beutler Ink, TechSpot, Metric37, humanizeai.com.

## 1. Banned vocabulary

Pick the plain word, or cut the phrase.

- **Classic AI words:** delve, tapestry, testament, intricate, intricacies, interplay, pivotal, crucial, vital, key (as an adjective), paramount, underscore, highlight (as a verb), showcase, emphasize, bolster, garner, foster, enhance, elevate, empower, streamline, leverage, harness, unlock, unleash, navigate, embark, spearhead, align with, resonate, realm, landscape, ecosystem, journey, beacon, cornerstone, linchpin, multifaceted, nuanced, meticulous, meticulously, comprehensive, holistic, robust, seamless, dynamic, vibrant, enduring, profound, invaluable, valuable insights, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, game-changer, groundbreaking, transformative, revolutionize, synergy, paradigm, commendable, noteworthy, ever-evolving, fast-paced, results-driven, proven track record, passionate, thrilled, excited to.
- **2026 filler words** (Forbes, tropes.fyi): quietly, shift, matters, shape, land ("that landed"), actually, real ("the real problem"), earn, the work, hold, pull, compound, signal, load-bearing, built different, unpack, lean into, double down, move the needle.
- **Magic adverbs** that make ordinary things sound significant: quietly, deeply, fundamentally, remarkably, arguably, genuinely, truly, incredibly, notably, significantly, ultimately, essentially.
- **Stacked connectors:** Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, In addition, Consequently, Notably, Importantly, Indeed. Never start back-to-back sentences with them. Usually drop them.
- **Copula dodging:** "serves as", "stands as", "acts as", "functions as", "boasts", "features" when you mean "is" or "has".

## 2. Banned phrases and sentence templates

- **Negative parallelism (contrast reframe):** "It's not X, it's Y", "This isn't about X. It's about Y", "Not only X but also Y", "Less X, more Y", "No X. No Y. Just Z." Say what the thing is without first knocking down a claim nobody made.
- **Rule of three:** "fast, reliable, and scalable". Use two, or four, or one. One three-item list per paragraph at most. Three lists in a row is a strong tell.
- **Superficial "-ing" tails:** ", highlighting the importance of", ", underscoring its role in", ", reflecting a broader trend", ", contributing to", ", ensuring", ", paving the way for", ", cementing its status as", ", showcasing". Give the concrete effect its own sentence with a subject, or delete it.
- **Inflated significance and legacy:** "plays a vital/pivotal role", "is a testament to", "leaves a lasting impact", "marks a significant milestone", "a key turning point", "rich history", "deeply rooted", "enduring legacy", "setting the stage for", "at the forefront of".
- **Promotional or brochure language:** nestled, in the heart of, breathtaking, stunning, must-visit, hidden gem, boasts a, world-class, renowned, rich cultural heritage.
- **Editorializing signposts:** "It's important to note that", "It's worth noting", "It is crucial to remember", "Needless to say", "Interestingly".
- **Ta-da and fake-suspense lines:** "Here's the thing", "Here's the kicker", "But here's the truth", "Here's what nobody tells you", "The uncomfortable truth is", "Let that sink in", "Spoiler:", "Plot twist:", "The best part?", "The result?", and any rhetorical question answered in the next line.
- **Framing clichés:** "In today's fast-paced world", "In a world where", "In the ever-evolving landscape of", "Imagine a world where", "Think of it as", "At its core", "When it comes to", "In the realm of", "Let's dive in", "Let's break it down", "Buckle up".
- **False ranges:** "from X to Y" where X and Y aren't two ends of a real scale ("from startups to enterprises", "from design to deployment").
- **Vague attributions (weasel words):** "Experts say", "Industry reports suggest", "Observers note", "Studies show", "Many believe", "It is widely recognized". Name the source or drop the claim.
- **Stakes inflation:** calling a small change a revolution ("This changes everything", "a paradigm shift").
- **Reassurance tics:** "And that's okay", "You're not alone", "Trust the process".
- **Hedged non-answers:** "It depends on various factors", "There are many considerations". Give the answer.

## 3. Structure and rhythm

- **Vary sentence length.** Rows of 15 to 20 word sentences are what detectors measure first (low "burstiness"). Put a 5-word sentence next to a 25-word one.
- **Vary paragraph length.** Avoid a row of same-size blocks that each end on a tidy summary line.
- **Don't open by restating the question or close by restating the answer.** Lead with the answer and stop when done.
- **No summary closers:** "In summary", "In conclusion", "Overall", "Ultimately", "All in all", "At the end of the day", "The bottom line".
- **No "Challenges and Future Prospects" wrap-up:** "Despite its successes, X faces challenges... Looking ahead, X is poised to...". Wikipedia calls this formula out by name.
- **No upbeat moral** at the end of a paragraph or message.
- **No fractal summaries:** an intro that previews, a body, then a conclusion that repeats the preview.
- **No elegant variation.** Once something has a name, keep using it. Don't rotate "the company", "the firm", "the organization".
- **Don't start consecutive sentences the same way** ("I built... I led... I designed...").
- **No anaphora for drama** ("We built it. We shipped it. We owned it.").
- **No strings of one-line paragraphs** for punch, LinkedIn style.
- **Pick specifics over abstractions:** a number, a name, a date, a tool. AI writes around the concrete detail. People write about it.

## 4. Punctuation and formatting

- **No em dashes (—).** Use a comma, full stop, colon, or parentheses. En dashes in date ranges are fine.
- **Straight quotes and apostrophes** (" and ') in plain-text output, not curly ones.
- **No stray bold** mid-sentence.
- **No bold-label bullets** ("**Scalability:** The system scales...") in messages or emails.
- **Sentence case headings** in casual writing, not Title Case.
- **No emoji** as bullets or decoration unless the user asks.
- **No Markdown in plain-text destinations** (emails, messages, LinkedIn, web forms): no `**`, `#`, backticks, or `-` bullets.
- **Don't turn everything into a list.** If two sentences do the job, write two sentences.
- **One colon or semicolon pivot per paragraph** at most.

## 5. Chatbot artifacts (must never reach a deliverable)

- **Assistant chatter:** "I hope this helps", "Certainly!", "Of course!", "Great question!", "Absolutely!", "You're absolutely right", "Let me know if you'd like...", "Feel free to...", "Would you like me to...", "Here is a revised version:".
- **Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers:** "As of my last update", "As of my knowledge cutoff", "I may not have the latest information".
- **AI self-reference or refusals:** "As an AI language model", "I cannot browse".
- **Placeholders:** "[Your Name]", "[Company]", "XX%", "INSERT DATE". Fill them in or ask.
- **Letter templates:** "Subject:" lines or "Dear Hiring Manager," unless the medium needs them.
- **Citation debris:** `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, `oaicite`, `contentReference`, `turn0search0`, made-up references or URLs.
- **Sycophancy:** flattering the reader, the company, or the role before saying anything.
